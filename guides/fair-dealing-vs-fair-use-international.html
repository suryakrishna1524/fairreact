Fair Use vs. Fair Dealing: International Copyright Laws for Creators

By Surya Krishna • Published September 2026 • 10 min read

While most online discussions focus on United States Fair Use (17 U.S.C. § 107), digital content uploaded to YouTube is distributed worldwide. In Commonwealth jurisdictions like the United Kingdom, India, Canada, and Australia, copyright exceptions are governed by the statutory doctrine of Fair Dealing.

1. The Fundamental Difference: Open vs. Closed Categories

The primary distinction between US Fair Use and international Fair Dealing lies in statutory flexibility:

  • US Fair Use (Open Standard): The law provides general illustrative purposes (criticism, comment, teaching) but allows courts to find fair use in novel contexts based on a flexible four-factor balancing test.
  • Commonwealth Fair Dealing (Exhaustive/Closed List): The use MUST strictly fall within one of the explicitly enumerated statutory categories (e.g. criticism, review, news reporting, or private research). If your video does not meet an enumerated purpose, fair dealing defense is legally unavailable regardless of fairness.

2. Fair Dealing in India (Copyright Act, 1957)

Under Section 52(1)(a) of the Indian Copyright Act, fair dealing is protected specifically for:

  • Private or personal use, including research;
  • Criticism or review, whether of that work or of any other work;
  • Reporting of current events and current affairs.

Indian courts require that the user genuinely intended to criticize or review the work, rather than create a competing entertainment substitute.

3. Fair Dealing in the United Kingdom & Canada

In the UK (CDPA 1988) and Canada (Copyright Act), criticism and review require sufficient acknowledgement of the author and title. Furthermore, the work being reviewed must have already been made available to the public with author consent.
